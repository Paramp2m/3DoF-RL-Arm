RL-ARM

A robot arm that learned five tasks by reinforcement learning, in simulation.

See the results

A three-joint arm in MuJoCo learned to reach, hold, track a moving target, avoid a box and push a cube. 88.7 to 100% success, three seeds each.

It sees its joint angles, joint speeds, the tip and the target. Every 25th of a second it nudges each joint's setpoint. Nobody wrote the motion for the first three tasks; the obstacle task also uses a planner waypoint.

The clip above follows a moving target: the trained hold policy, 14.7 mm mean tip-to-target distance over the second half of 2000 headless steps. The clip here is the opposite case, a classical IK controller with no learning.

What did not work

Network only, best of six
89.7% success, 11.3% collisions
Kept policy
88.7% success, 1.7% collisions
Planner alone
89% success, 3% collisions

I wanted the network alone to learn the detour around the box, so I tried six ways: a planner as teacher, imitation, then reinforcement fine-tuning. The best hit the box in 11.3% of episodes, against a limit of 5%.

The policy I kept leans on a planner waypoint and a safety filter. A planner alone scores almost the same, so the learning adds little there. The comparisons are early too: at 300k steps every curve was still rising.

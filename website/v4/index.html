An arm thatlearned five tasks

Reach, hold, track, avoid a box, push a cube, learned with reinforcement learning in simulation. 88.7 to 100% success, three seeds each.

See the results

Each policy learns by trying, missing and being scored, for up to a million steps.

It sees its joint angles, joint speeds, the tip and the target. Its answer is a small change to each joint's position setpoint, 25 times a second.

The clip above follows a moving target: the trained hold policy, 14.7 mm mean tip-to-target distance over the second half of 2000 headless steps.

What failed

The network alone

I tried six ways to make the network avoid the box without help: a planner as teacher, imitation, then SAC fine-tuning. The best reached 89.7% success but collided in 11.3% of episodes, against a 5% limit.

What I kept

A planner waypoint, a learned residual and a safety filter: 88.7% success, 1.7% collisions. A planner alone gets 89% and 3%, so the learning adds little there.
